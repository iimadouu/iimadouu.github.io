The approach

What this actually looks like.

Selling online in Algeria is not the same as selling in Europe. Buyers expect cash on delivery, they order from a phone on a slow connection, and they often confirm by phone. A checkout designed elsewhere will lose you orders before you ever see them.

I build stores and marketplaces around those realities: a catalogue that is easy to manage, a two-step checkout, delivery and wilaya handling, and an admin area where you can see what is actually happening. Payment integration is added where it helps rather than forced on every order.

For marketplaces, each vendor gets a dashboard, products are reviewed before they appear, commissions are calculated automatically, and you keep the analytics that show which vendors and categories deserve more attention.

Questions

Asked, answered.

Can customers pay cash on delivery?

Yes. Cash on delivery with an order-confirmation step is built in, alongside online payment for customers who prefer it.

Can I start with a normal store and grow into a marketplace?

Yes. The catalogue and checkout are built so vendor accounts, dashboards and commissions can be added later without rebuilding the store.

How much does an online store cost in Algeria?

A basic store with a catalogue, cart and checkout typically starts around 225,000 DZD. A multi-vendor platform is usually 540,000–900,000 DZD, and an advanced marketplace with commissions, reviews and analytics ranges from 1,125,000 to 1,800,000 DZD.

Can you connect a payment provider?

Yes, local and international providers depending on what your business can use. Payment confirmation happens on the server so orders cannot be faked.

Do you handle the products and photos?

I build the store and import your catalogue. Product photography and copywriting can be arranged separately, or you supply the content and I structure and optimise it.
